TITLE: Assessing the clinical utility of composite outcomes in studies examining outcomes of implantable neuromodulation therapies for pain: a systematic review

ABSTRACT:
Implantable neuromodulation therapies are offered to patients with certain refractory pain syndromes. These therapies are resource-intensive and effort-intensive and may be associated with significant adverse effects. Change in pain intensity score, an unidimensional measurement tool, is currently the most used eligibility criteria for patients to receive implanted neuromodulation devices. However, pain is a biopsychosocial phenomenon, and assessment of effectiveness of neuromodulation therapies using tools that incorporate multiple pain-related domains may be more relevant and accurate. Composite measures integrate multiple domains of patient well-being, enabling a clinically relevant assessment of treatment effects. This systematic review aims to evaluate the literature on the clinical utility and reliability of composite outcomes as a means to assess efficacy of implantable neuromodulation therapies for refractory pain.
We will search Embase, MEDLINE, Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials and the WHO’s International Clinical Trials Registry Platform. Searches will be limited to from inception of each database to 31 December 2025. Studies published in English will be considered eligible if they used composite outcomes to evaluate the efficacy and/or effectiveness of implantable neuromodulation therapies for treating refractory pain. The studies should investigate adult populations (aged ≥18 years) undergoing implantation for chronic refractory pain of moderate-to-severe intensity. Two reviewers will independently screen articles, extract data and review the risk of bias and the grade of evidence provided in the studies. Extracted data will include study details (author, year, country of origin), participant demographics (age, sex), sample sizes and intervention details. Outcome measures include pain intensity, physical health, mental health, quality of life, medication use and neuromodulation device explantation rates. Data will be collected at baseline, 3 months, 6 months and 12 months post-implantation where available. A meta-analysis will be formed if there is sufficient homogeneity in the studies and their data.
As this study is a systematic review using data that has already been published in scientific literature or is publicly available, ethics approval is not required. For dissemination, we plan to share the findings of our review through multiple academic and clinical channels. The completed review will be submitted for publication in a peer-reviewed journal relevant to pain medicine or neuromodulation.

## Background

Implantable neuromodulation therapies involve the use of electrical or chemical energy to ameliorate pain refractory to conventional medical management. These therapies include deep brain stimulation, spinal cord stimulation (SCS), peripheral nerve stimulation and intrathecal drug delivery (IDD) systems used to treat a variety of pain syndromes, such as complex regional pain syndromes, persistent spinal pain syndrome (PSPS), neuropathic pain and ischaemic pain, from various aetiologies. Implants of neuromodulation systems are often preceded by a trial, during which a 50% reduction in pain intensity is usually required for the patient to be offered a SCS implant. However, pain intensity scores are unidimensional and not a measure of a broader, patient-centred outcome incorporating physical health, mental well-being and social function.

The question of whether pain intensity alone is sufficient to capture the full scope of impact and changes in the quality of life observed in patients with implantable neuromodulation therapies has been raised in a few publications. Furthermore, patient satisfaction with treatment for pain has been recognised to be of great importance in evaluating therapies for pain. Given the persistent and complex nature of pain-related conditions, achieving long-term improvement across all domains of a patient’s life, not just physiological pain relief, may hold greater clinical relevance in the context of implantable neuromodulation therapies for pain.

Composite outcomes combine two or more dimensions into a single metric and these have been proposed and evaluated in other areas of medicine such as treatments for cardiovascular and neurological conditions. By summarising multiple domains of patient well-being, composite measures allow for the integration of clinically relevant evaluation of treatment effects, particularly when multiple outcomes are of interest. One of the best-known examples of composite outcomes in medicine is MACE (major adverse cardiovascular events), a composite of adverse cardiovascular events including myocardial infarction, stroke and cardiovascular death. It is used frequently in cardiovascular research, and it provides an overarching view of treatment impact by encompassing multiple clinically significant outcomes. In the study of pain and neuromodulation therapies, however, there is a gap in research and practice surrounding the use of composite outcomes. This systematic review aims to evaluate the literature on the clinical utility and reliability of composite outcomes as a means to assess the effectiveness of implantable neuromodulation therapies for pain management as compared with pain intensity scores.

## Materials and methods

We drafted the protocol according to the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis (PRISMA) Protocols 2015 and registered the protocol in the PROSPERO database under the registration number CRD42025631488. A completed PRISMA checklist has been provided as .

## Patient and public involvement

Patients and members of the public were not involved in the design, conduct, reporting or dissemination plans of this research.

## Literature search

We will search the databases of Embase, MEDLINE and Cochrane, and the WHO clinical trial registry to look for all registered studies which fulfil our eligibility criteria. Our search will be restricted to studies in the databases from the inception of each of the databases to 31 December 2025. A search for MEDLINE is provided as an example in .

## Eligibility criteria

Studies are eligible if they are prospective cohort studies, retrospective cohort studies or contrasting studies published in the English language, which have used composite outcomes to evaluate the efficacy and/or effectiveness of implantable neuromodulation therapies as an intervention for treating refractory pain. These therapies include deep brain stimulation, SCS, peripheral nerve stimulation or IDD systems. The studies should look at adult patient populations (18 years and older) who have undergone implants of neuromodulation therapies for the treatment of chronic refractory pain of moderate-to-severe intensity (pain intensity 4 or higher on a 0–10 numerical rating scale or equivalent). We intend to include any studies in which implantable neuromodulation therapies were performed for pain. This would include pain secondary to neuropathy, vascular aetiologies, complex regional pain syndrome and other causes. Studies focusing on paediatric populations or non-pain-related indications will be excluded. We will also exclude studies in which IDD with baclofen was provided for spasticity.

## Study screening

Paired reviewers will conduct study selection in two stages. The first stage will involve screening titles and abstracts to exclude studies that do not meet the eligibility criteria. In the second stage, full texts will be reviewed for inclusion of the studies based on the eligibility criteria, and any disagreements between reviewers will be resolved through discussion or, if necessary, by involving a third reviewer. To assess inter-rater reliability, a kappa agreement score will be calculated and reported during the full-text screening phase. A PRISMA flow diagram of our study selection process is provided in .

## Data extraction

Data extraction will be performed independently by paired reviewers using piloted electronic forms, with an accompanying instruction manual to ensure consistency. The extracted data will include study details (eg, author, year of publication, country of origin), participant demographics (eg, age, sex), the number of participants included in the analysis and details of the intervention procedure. Additionally, all scores reported in the studies related to pain outcome domains will be extracted. These domains include pain intensity (eg, Numerical Rating Scale (NRS), Visual Analogue Scale (VAS)), physical health (eg, Oswestry Disability Index (ODI), Brief Pain Inventory, Roland-Morris Disability Questionnaire (RMDQ) or objective markers such as steps walked), mental health (eg, General Anxiety Disorder-7 for anxiety, Patient Health Questionnaire-9 for depression, Pain Catastrophising Scale and similar measures), quality of life (eg, EuroQol-5D, the 36-Item Short Form Survey Instrument, medication use (eg, opioid dosage or oral morphine equivalents) and neuromodulation device explantation or failure to provide analgesia rates. We are especially interested in the outcome of explantation rates of the neuromodulation implant therapies, given the fact that explantation rates are used to determine the success or failure rate of the neuromodulation implants at a population level.

The data to be collected from each study included in the review can be found in . Data will be collected for baseline, 3 months, 6 months and 12 months post-implantation, depending on score availability from each study. The specific measures for pain outcomes will vary based on the individual study. If any of the eligible studies are missing some of the data we plan to extract, we will reach out to the corresponding authors to obtain the necessary data. Reviewers will treat missing data with the intention-to-treat principle.

## Risk-of-bias assessments

We will be using the revised Cochrane risk-of-bias tool to assess studies for their randomisation process, completion of outcome data, allocation concealment and selection of reported outcomes. The risk of publication bias will be assessed using a funnel plot. If study results exhibit heavy tails or skewness, Begg’s rank test will be applied. Otherwise, Egger’s regression test will be used to evaluate publication bias.

## Strategy for data synthesis

The characteristics of the studies included will be summarised in a comprehensive table. For dichotomous outcomes, we intend to estimate the relative effects using relative risks and their 95% CIs and calculate the interventions’ absolute effects. For continuous outcomes that vary largely across populations, we intend to estimate the relative effects using the ratio of means and their 95% CIs. We intend to calculate mean differences and their 95% CIs for the post-intervention scores of other continuous outcomes. Meta-analyses may be performed using RevMan software and Forest plots may be used to evaluate the predictive accuracy of successful outcomes for SCS trials and implants with composite outcomes as compared with pain scores. For direct comparisons of outcomes, when there are at least two trials, we may conduct DerSimonian-Laird random-effects meta-analyses. Data synthesis would depend on the quality of data available and homogeneity of the data.

To enable comparisons between groups, all pain intensity scores (eg, NRS or VAS) will be standardised to a 0–10 scale. The mean and SD for these outcomes will be calculated at baseline, 3 months, 6 months and 12 months post-implantation. However, in cases where scores are not directly synthesisable across studies within a specific pain outcome domain, alternative strategies will be employed. For example, if studies use different measures (eg, ODI vs RMDQ), data will be categorised by the extent of change in scores (eg, no change, mild change, moderate change), and the number of participants in each category will be quantified.

## Analysis of subgroups or subsets

If available, we will conduct a subgroup analysis on the differences between studies that evaluated SCS for pain and those that evaluated IDD studies. For patients with PSPS, a subgroup analysis will be conducted to examine changes in both pain scores and composite outcomes. Furthermore, depending on the availability of studies, we may conduct a subgroup analysis on studies that used composite outcomes that do or do not incorporate quality of life questionnaires.

## Certainty of evidence

To assess the certainty of evidence, we will use the Grading of Recommendations, Assessment, Development and Evaluations approach. We will start with high certainty for direct evidence and rate it down considering the risk of bias, inconsistency, indirectness, imprecision and publication bias. We will evaluate heterogeneity among trials in each comparison using the I 2 statistic, with I statistic, with I 2 ≥50% indicating a substantial heterogeneity and consider the context when assessing inconsistency. ≥50% indicating a substantial heterogeneity and consider the context when assessing inconsistency.

## Discussion

Currently, there is no standardised composite outcome used to assess the efficacy of implantable neuromodulation therapies for refractory pain. Eligibility for these therapies remains heavily reliant on unidimensional pain scale scores, which do not fully capture the complex biopsychosocial nature of chronic pain. Pain intensity alone does not account for the broader impact of pain on a patient’s physical, mental and social well-being. This systematic review aims to address this gap by evaluating the clinical utility and reliability of composite outcomes that integrate multiple pain-related domains. By synthesising available evidence, this review will provide insight into whether composite measures offer a more comprehensive and clinically meaningful assessment of neuromodulation therapy outcomes. Findings from this review could inform future clinical guidelines and contribute to the development of more nuanced and patient-centred assessment tools.

## Ethics and dissemination

As this study is a systematic review using data that has already been published in scientific literature or is publicly available, ethics approval is not required. For dissemination, we plan to share the findings of our review through multiple academic and clinical channels. The completed review will be submitted for publication in a peer-reviewed journal relevant to pain medicine or neuromodulation. We may also present the results at scientific conferences in these fields. In addition, we aim to explore opportunities to incorporate the evidence into digital decision-support platforms such as the MAGICapp, a novel electronic format for evidence summaries and decision aids.
